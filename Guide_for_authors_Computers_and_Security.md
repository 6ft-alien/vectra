# Guide for Authors — Computers & Security (ISSN 0167-4048), Elsevier

## About the Journal

### Aims and Scope

**The International Source of Innovation for the Information Security and IT Audit Professional**

Computers & Security is one of the most respected journals in IT security, being recognized worldwide as THE primary source of reference for IT security research and applications expertise.

Computers & Security provides the IT security community with a unique blend of leading edge research and sound practical management advice. It is aimed at the professional involved with computer security, audit, control and data integrity in all sectors — industry, commerce and academia — helping the community build and operate fully secure systems and organisations.

With its high-profile editorial board and informative regular features and columns, the journal is essential reading for IT security professionals around the world.

- Our cutting edge research will help you secure and maintain your systems
- We accept only the highest quality of papers ensuring that you receive the relevant and practical advice you need
- We don't only highlight the threats, we give you the solutions

> **Cryptology:** The journal's editorial scope has explicitly excluded cryptology since 2006. Therefore, submissions with some aspect of cryptology (including blockchains, watermarking, and steganography) as a principal component will not be considered for review.

> **AI and ML:** As of early 2024, we have instituted a moratorium on consideration of submissions that feature AI or ML as significant components. Thus, submissions about applying an AI/ML technique to system security and privacy topics will not be considered. Also, items directed to the security of AI/ML systems themselves (such as LLM and federated learning) are out of scope of the journal and should be submitted to a venue primarily about AI/ML.

### Peer Review

This journal follows a single anonymized review process. Your submission will initially be assessed by our editors. If your submission is deemed suitable, it will typically be sent to be reviewed by a minimum of two reviewers for an independent expert assessment of the scientific quality.

The decision as to whether your article is accepted or rejected for publication will be made by our editors.

Our editors are not involved in making decisions about papers which:
- they have written themselves
- have been written by family members or colleagues
- relate to products or services in which they have an interest

Any such submissions will be subject to the journal's usual procedures and peer review will be handled independently of the editor involved and their research group.

Authors may submit a formal appeal request to the editorial decision, provided it meets all the requirements and follows the procedure outlined in Elsevier's Appeal Policy. Only one appeal per submission will be considered and the appeal decision will be final.

**Special issues and article collections:** The peer review process for special issues and article collections follows the same process as outlined above for regular submissions, except a guest editor may send the submissions out to the reviewers and may recommend a decision to the journal editor. The journal editor oversees the peer review process of all special issues and article collections to ensure the high standards of publishing ethics and responsiveness are respected and is responsible for the final decision regarding acceptance or rejection of articles.

## Ethics and Policies

### Ethics in Publishing

Authors must follow ethical guidelines stated in Elsevier's Publishing Ethics Policy.

### Submission Declaration

When authors submit an article to an Elsevier journal it is implied that:
- The work described has not been published previously except in the form of a preprint, an abstract, a published lecture, academic thesis or registered report.
- The article is not under consideration for publication elsewhere.
- The article's publication is approved by all authors and tacitly or explicitly by the responsible authorities where the work was carried out.
- If accepted, the article will not be published elsewhere in the same form, in English or in any other language, including electronically, without the written consent of the copyright-holder.

To verify compliance with journal publishing policies, submissions may be checked with screening tools.

### Authorship

All authors should have made substantial contributions to all of the following:
1. The conception and design of the study, or acquisition of data, or analysis and interpretation of data
2. Drafting the article or revising it critically for important intellectual content
3. Final approval of the version to be submitted

All authors are accountable for all aspects of the work, to ensure that questions related to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated and resolved.

**Corresponding author:** Authors must appoint a single corresponding author to communicate with the journal during the editorial process. This individual submits the manuscript and all required documentation to the journal, and serves as the primary contact between the journal and the co-authors. All contact details of the corresponding author must be kept up to date throughout the submission and publication process. Only the corresponding author's affiliation will be used to determine eligibility for a publishing agreement and possible related discounts. Affiliations of other co-authors are not relevant for eligibility.

**Group authorship:** Group authorship refers to the attribution of an article to a defined and named group of authors whose members have contributed to the work (e.g., research consortia, clinical trial groups, collaborative networks, task forces, expert panels).

- Group authorship must be declared on the title page at submission, under "Collaborative Author Groups" in the submission system.
- A corresponding author who agrees to coordinate all communications on behalf of the group must be designated.
- The group name will be included in the author list in the published article (e.g., "on behalf of the [Consortium Name]").
- All group members must be listed in a dedicated section, preferably an appendix.
- All listed authors must follow the journal's authorship and authorship change policies.
- The group must maintain a transparent internal process for determining authorship and resolving disputes; all authorship disputes must be resolved within the group.

### Changes to Authorship

The editors generally will not consider changes to authorship once a manuscript has been submitted. Authors should carefully consider the authorship list and order at submission.

- All authors must be listed in the manuscript and their details entered into the submission system. Changes (additions, deletions, or rearrangement) can only be made prior to acceptance, and only with journal editor approval.
- Requests must be made by the corresponding author using the Authorship Change Request form, with written confirmation from all authors (including those being added or removed).
- No authorship changes are allowed after acceptance.
- The review process may be paused while a change request is being considered.
- Non-compliant change requests may result in rejection, or retraction if already published.

### Declaration of Competing Interests

All authors must disclose any financial and personal relationships that could inappropriately influence or bias their work, including:
- Employment
- Consultancies
- Stock ownership
- Honoraria
- Paid expert testimony
- Patent applications or registrations
- Grants or any other funding
- Affiliation with the journal as an Editor or Advisory Board Member

The declarations tool should always be completed. Authors with a journal affiliation to declare should enter specified disclosure text under "Other Activities" and inform the journal/publisher prior to completing submission. Authors with no competing interests should select "I have nothing to declare." The resulting Word document must be uploaded during submission in .doc/.docx format; author signatures are not required.

### Funding Sources

Authors must disclose any funding sources supporting the research and/or article preparation, and declare the role (if any) of sponsors in study design, data collection/analysis/interpretation, writing, and the decision to submit.

Standard funding statement format:
> Funding: This work was supported by the National Institutes of Health [grant numbers xxxx, yyyy]; the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, Seattle, WA [grant number zzzz]; and the United States Institutes of Peace [grant number aaaa].

If no funding was received:
> This research did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors.

### Declaration of Generative AI Use

Authors must declare the use of generative AI tools in manuscript preparation upon submission. AI tools may support authors but must never substitute for human critical thinking, expertise and evaluation, and may only be applied with human oversight and control.

Authors are responsible and accountable for:
- Carefully reviewing and verifying the accuracy, comprehensiveness, and impartiality of all AI-generated output (including checking sources, as AI-generated references can be incorrect or fabricated)
- Editing and adapting all material to ensure the manuscript reflects the author's authentic and original contribution
- Ensuring accuracy and originality of any AI-generated images with appropriate attribution
- Making transparent to readers the use of any AI-based or other tools/sources, via a disclosure statement upon submission
- Ensuring the manuscript safeguards data privacy, intellectual property and other rights

AI tools must not be listed or cited as an author/co-author.

**Required statement**, placed in a new section before the references list:

> **Title of new section:** Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process.
>
> **Statement:** During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [NAME OF TOOL / SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the published article.

This declaration does not apply to basic tools (grammar/spelling/reference checkers) or to assistive technology used solely for accessibility. If nothing to disclose, no statement is needed.

Academic editors and reviewers are not currently permitted to upload unpublished manuscripts into generative AI tools, though AI may be used in a supportive capacity to improve structure/language of review reports or decision letters, or for background literature searches.

### Preprints

Authors may share preprints (e.g., on a preprint server) in line with Elsevier's article sharing policy; this does not count as prior publication.

This journal offers a free preprint posting service on SSRN. During submission, authors can choose to post the manuscript on SSRN, made publicly available once it passes initial desk review. Benefits include early registration with a preprint DOI, a link to the version of record if published, and early citation opportunities. The decision to post has no effect on editorial process or outcome. The corresponding author should seek approval from all co-authors before posting publicly on SSRN prior to peer review.

### Use of Inclusive Language

Work should use inclusive language throughout and contain nothing implying one individual is superior to another on grounds of age, gender, race, ethnicity, culture, sexual orientation, or disability/health condition. Avoid descriptors about personal attributes unless relevant and valid; prefer gender-neutral plural nouns over "he, she," or "he/she." Writing should be free from bias, stereotypes, slang, and unwarranted cultural assumptions.

### Reporting Sex- and Gender-Based Analyses

There is no single, universally agreed-upon set of guidelines for defining sex and gender. Guidance includes:
- Sex- and gender-based analyses (SGBA) should be integrated into research design when research involves or pertains to humans, animals or eukaryotic cells, per funder/sponsor requirements and field best practices.
- Sex and/or gender dimensions should be addressed in the article or declared as a limitation to generalizability.
- Definitions of sex and/or gender applied should be explicitly stated for precision, rigor and reproducibility.

Authors are encouraged to consult the SAGER (Sex and Gender Equity in Research) guidelines and checklist on the EASE website.

**Definitions:**
- **Sex** generally refers to biological attributes (chromosomal genotype, hormonal levels, internal/external anatomy), typically designated at birth, recognizing that sex categorizations include intersex/DSD individuals.
- **Gender** generally refers to socially constructed roles, behaviors and identities that vary across societies and time, influencing self-view, interaction, and power distribution.

Where relevant, authors must clearly explain how the paper and research define and use sex and gender.

### Image Manipulation

Adjusting images for clarity is accepted; manipulation for deception or fraud is treated as scientific ethical abuse.

Policy for graphical images:
- No specific feature within an image may be enhanced, obscured, moved, removed or introduced.
- Adjustments of brightness, contrast, or color balance are acceptable only as long as they do not obscure or eliminate information present in the original image.
- Nonlinear adjustments (e.g., gamma settings) must be disclosed in the figure legend.
- Use of AI tools to create or manipulate primary research images (e.g., western blots, microscopy, histology, patient images) is not permitted.

Images may be sent to a secure third-party tool to screen for irregularities; editors may request original data/images. The final decision on acceptability rests with the editors. Authors are encouraged to check all images before submission and connect figure data to original, unprocessed data.

### Jurisdictional Claims

Elsevier takes a neutral position on territorial disputes or jurisdictional claims, including maps and institutional affiliations (unless a third-party journal owner sets its own policy).

- **Maps:** Study areas should be locatable using common mapping platforms; maps should only show the area actually studied (not a larger bounding box), with a note stating that map lines delineate study areas and do not necessarily depict accepted national boundaries.
- **Institutional affiliations:** Use the full standard title or standard abbreviation of the institution so it can be independently verified.

### Delayed Publication

Contact the editorial office or the Journal Article Publishing Support Center as early as possible to request a publication delay; timely requests will be accommodated where possible, but this is not guaranteed. Once the agreed delay period expires, the article may be published at any time.

### Withdrawal, Correction, or Retraction

Consult Elsevier's Article Correction, Retraction and Removal Policy for questions on these topics.

## Writing and Formatting

### File Format

- Provide editable source files for the entire submission (including figures, tables and text graphics).
- Save files in an editable format: .doc/.docx for Word, .tex for LaTeX. A PDF is not an acceptable source file.
- Format Word files in a single-column layout (double-column formatting is only permitted for LaTeX submissions).
- Remove strikethrough and underlined text unless scientifically significant.
- Use spell-check and grammar-check functions.

### Title Page

Required details:
- **Article title:** concise and informative; avoid abbreviations and formulae unless established and widely understood (e.g., DNA).
- **Author names:** given name(s) and family name(s) of each author, in the order matching the submission system; own-script name may be added in parentheses.
- **Affiliations:** below author names, indicated by lower-case superscript letters, with full name and postal address including country.
- **Corresponding author:** clearly indicated, including email address (which will be published).
- **Present/permanent address:** indicated by footnote (superscript Arabic numeral) if affiliation has changed since the work was carried out; the address where the work was done remains the main affiliation.

### Abstract

A concise, factual abstract not exceeding 250 words that:
- States the purpose of the paper
- Outlines basic procedures followed
- Includes main findings (with statistical significance where applicable) and principal conclusions
- Emphasizes new and important aspects of the study

Guidelines: abstracts must stand alone; avoid references (if essential, cite author(s) and year(s)); avoid non-standard/uncommon abbreviations (define at first mention if essential).

### Keywords

Provide 5 to 10 keywords in English for indexing. Avoid multi-word keywords using "and" or "of." Use abbreviations only if firmly established in the field.

### Highlights

Encouraged at submission. Highlights capture novel results and new methods, aiding discoverability via search engines.
- Submit as a separate editable file with "highlights" in the file name.
- 3 to 5 bullet points, each a maximum of 85 characters (including spaces).

### Graphical Abstract

Encouraged at submission; summarizes the article concisely and pictorially for an interdisciplinary audience.
- Obtain permission for any third-party owned material.
- Use of generative AI must align with Elsevier's GenAI Policies for Journals.
- Submit as a separate file.
- Image should be 531 x 1328 pixels (h x w) or proportionally more, readable at 5 x 13 cm.
- Preferred file types: TIFF, EPS, PDF or MS Office files.

### Math Formulae

- Submit as editable text, not images.
- Present simple formulae in line with normal text where possible.
- Use a solidus (/) instead of a horizontal line for small fractions.
- Present variables in italics.
- Denote powers of e by exp.
- Display equations separately, numbered consecutively as referenced.

### Tables

- Submit as editable text, not images.
- Place next to relevant text or on separate page(s) at the end.
- Cite all tables in the text; number consecutively by appearance.
- Provide captions; place table notes below the table body.
- Avoid vertical rules and shading within cells.
- Use tables sparingly; avoid duplicating results described elsewhere.

### Figures, Images and Other Artwork

- Supply as separate files per artwork and media instructions.
- Cite all images in the text; number according to order of appearance.
- Submit each image as a separate file with a logical naming convention (e.g., Figure_1, Figure_2).
- Text graphics may be embedded at the appropriate position (also in LaTeX files).

**Captions:** All artwork must have a caption consisting of a brief title (not on the figure itself) and a description; keep text minimal, explain symbols/abbreviations.

**Formats:**

| Image type | Recommended file type | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Vector drawings | EPS, PDF | Embed the font or save text as graphics |
| Photographs (color or grayscale) | TIFF, JPG, PNG | Min. resolution 300 dpi (min. width: single column 1063 px; full page 2244 px) |
| Bitmapped line drawings | TIFF, JPG, PNG | Min. resolution 1000 dpi (min. width: single column 3543 px; full page 7480 px) |
| Charts and large or shaded tables | XLS, XLSX | Supply in a clearly readable format |
| Annotated images | PPT, PPTX | Annotations/text must be legible; embedded images min. 300 dpi |

**Do not submit:** files too low in resolution; artwork with disproportionately small text; multiple images/graphs combined into one file (except for intentional before/during/after comparisons).

**Color artwork:** Usable color figures will appear in color online. Ensure accessibility for readers with impaired color vision. For print, cost information for color reproduction will be sent after acceptance, at which point authors can choose online-only or online+print color.

### Generative AI and Figures, Images and Other Artwork

Per Elsevier's GenAI Policies for Journals:
- AI tools may be used to support creation of explanatory images (flow charts, conceptual diagrams, schematic illustrations).
- AI tools may be used to create data visualizations (plots, charts, graphs, heatmaps) when directly derived from underlying data using reproducible analytical/computational/statistical methods.
- AI tools must not be used to create or alter images representing primary observed or experimental data (e.g., western blots, microscopy, histology, patient images) not directly obtained in the research, including brightness/contrast/color adjustments (use established image processing software instead).
- This does not prevent AI use in formal research design or methods (e.g., AI-assisted imaging approaches for data collection/interpretation, or predictive modelling of clinical outcomes).

Authors are responsible for accuracy and originality of all submitted images. Disclose generative AI use for images in each image caption as well as in the general Generative AI disclosure statement.

### Supplementary Material

- Should be accurate and relevant to the research.
- Cite all supplementary files in the text.
- Submit all supplementary materials together with the manuscript.
- Include a concise, descriptive caption for each file.
- After submission, supplementary files can only be added/replaced at the revision stage.
- Files appear online exactly as received (not checked, formatted, or typeset).

### Video

- Include links to video/animation files within articles to support and enhance research; add a note in the text where the file should be placed.
- Label files clearly, with names directly related to content.
- Use recommended file formats; max 150 MB per file, 1 GB total.
- Provide a "still" image for each file (any frame or a separate image) as a standard icon/link.
- Provide descriptive text referring to video content for accessibility (also needed in print, since video/animation cannot be embedded there).
- All supplied video/animation files are published in the electronic version.

### Research Data

The journal supports storage of, access to and discovery of research data per Elsevier's research data policy. Research data includes results of observations/experimentation validating findings, and may include software, code, models, algorithms, protocols, methods, and other related materials.

**Research data deposit, citation and linking (Option C applies):**
- Deposit research data in a relevant data repository.
- Cite and link to the dataset in the article.
- If not possible, provide a statement explaining why data cannot be shared.

### Data Statement

Authors must state data availability at submission (required by some funders/institutions). If data is unavailable or unsuitable to post, state the reason (e.g., sensitive or confidential patient data). This statement appears with the published article on ScienceDirect.

### Data Linking

Linking to underlying data increases exposure and may lead to collaborations. Options:
- Provide a dataset link when prompted during submission.
- Some repositories automatically display a repository banner next to the published article.
- Link relevant data/entities within the article text using identifiers, format: Database: 12345 (e.g., TAIR: AT1G01020; CCDC: 734053; PDB: 1XFN).

### Co-submission of Related Data, Methods or Protocols

Authors are encouraged to co-submit a description of research data, methods, or protocols to *Data in Brief* or *MethodsX* at the same time as the regular manuscript. If both are accepted, they are linked together on ScienceDirect. Use the mandatory templates from the relevant journal, and submit via the "Attach files" page in Editorial Manager; it is automatically forwarded. A personalized Article Processing Charge is presented only after acceptance, based on individual context.

## Journal-Specific Information: Writing and Formatting

### Manuscript Length Requirements

Article length should be no longer than **12,000 words**, including Abstract, Main text, References and any Appendices. The abstract should not exceed 250 words.

### Article Structure

**Article sections:** Divide the manuscript into clearly defined, numbered sections (1.1, 1.1.1, 1.1.2, ... then 1.2, etc.). Use this numbering when cross-referencing (not "the text"). Subsections may have brief headings on a separate line. Do not include the abstract within section numbering.

**Introduction:** Clearly state the work's objectives; provide adequate background but avoid a detailed literature overview or summary of results.

**Methods:** Provide sufficient detail for independent reproduction.
- If a method has been previously published, summarize it and cite the original.
- If quoting directly from a previously published method, use quotation marks and cite the source.
- Describe any modifications made to existing methods.

**Results:** Should be clear, concise and reproducible (see sections on tables, artwork, supplementary material and research data).

**Discussion:** Explore the significance of results without repeating them; avoid extensive citation/discussion of published literature here.

**Conclusion:** Present the main conclusions, either as a stand-alone section or as part of the discussion/results-and-discussion section.

**Theory and calculation:** The theory section extends the introduction's background; the calculation section represents a practical development from the theoretical basis.

**Acknowledgements:** Include individuals who helped with the research (e.g., language, writing, proofreading) in a separate section directly before the reference list — not on the title page or elsewhere.

**Author contributions (CRediT):** Corresponding authors must acknowledge co-author contributions using CRediT roles: Conceptualization, Data curation, Formal analysis, Funding acquisition, Investigation, Methodology, Project administration, Resources, Software, Supervision, Validation, Visualization, Writing – original draft, Writing – review and editing. Not all roles apply to every manuscript; authors may hold multiple roles.

**Appendices:**
- Identify individual appendices as A, B, etc.
- Number formulae/equations separately per appendix: Eq. (A.1), Eq. (A.2)...; Eq. (B.1), Eq. (B.2)...
- Number tables and figures similarly: Table A.1; Fig. A.1, etc.

**Vitae:** Submit a short biography (max 100 words) per author plus a passport-type photograph as a separate figure, in an editable format (e.g., Word, not PDF).

### References

**References within text:**
- Every reference cited must appear in the reference list, and vice versa.
- References cited in the abstract must be given in full.
- Ensure all reference list data is correct and complete; every reference must correspond to a real, existing source.
- Include DOIs where available.
- Unpublished results/personal communications are best mentioned in text rather than the reference list; if included, follow the standard reference style, substituting the date with "unpublished results" or "personal communication."
- "In press" citations imply acceptance for publication.

**Reference format:** No strict formatting required at submission; style must be consistent and include author names, journal/book titles, chapter/article titles, year, volume, article number or pagination as applicable. The journal's reference style is applied at proof stage after acceptance.

**Reference style (in-text citations):**
- Single author: author's name (without initials, unless ambiguous) and year.
- Two authors: both authors' names and year.
- Three or more authors: first author's name + 'et al.' and year.
- Example: "as demonstrated (Allan, 2020a, 2020b; Allan and Jones, 2019)" or "as demonstrated (Jones, 2019; Allan, 2020). Kramer et al. (2023) have recently shown".
- Reference list: arranged alphabetically then chronologically; multiple references from the same author(s)/year distinguished by 'a', 'b', 'c', etc.
- Use shortened last page numbers (e.g., 51–9); for more than 6 authors, list the first 6 followed by 'et al.'
- Abbreviate journal names per the List of Title Word Abbreviations (LTWA).

**Examples:**

- *Journal publication:*
  Van der Geer J, Handgraaf T, Lupton RA. The art of writing a scientific article. J Sci Commun 2020;163:51–9. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sc.2020.00372.

- *Journal publication with article number:*
  Van der Geer J, Handgraaf T, Lupton RA. The art of writing a scientific article. Heliyon. 2022;19:e00205. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2022.e00205.

- *Book:*
  Strunk Jr W, White EB. The elements of style. 4th ed. New York: Longman; 2000.

- *Book chapter:*
  Mettam GR, Adams LB. How to prepare an electronic version of your article. In: Jones BS, Smith RZ, editors. Introduction to the electronic age. New York: E-Publishing Inc; 2020. p. 281–304.

- *Website:*
  Cancer Research UK, Cancer statistics reports for the UK. http://www.cancerresearchuk.org/aboutcancer/statistics/cancerstatsreport/, 2023 (accessed 13 March 2023).

- *Dataset:*
  Oguro M, Imahiro S, Saito S, Nakashizuka T. Mortality data for Japanese oak wilt disease and surrounding forest compositions [dataset]. Mendeley Data. 2015. https://doi.org/10.17632/xwj98nb39r.1.

- *Software:*
  Coon E, Berndt M, Jan A, Svyatsky D, Atchley A, Kikinzon E, et al. Advanced Terrestrial Simulator (ATS). Version 0.88 [software]. Zenodo; 2020 Mar 25. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3727209.

**Web references:** Provide at minimum the full URL and date last accessed; include DOI, author names, dates, or source publication where known. List separately under a new heading after the reference list, or include within it.

**Data references:** Include author name(s), dataset title, data repository, version (if available), year, and global persistent identifier. Add "[dataset]" immediately before the reference (not shown in the published article).

**Software references:** Cite software (including code, scripts, models, notebooks, libraries) like other sources, including:
- Creator(s)
- Title
- Publication venue (preferably an archive/repository with permanent identifiers)
- Date (release/version date, or "n.d." if unknown)
- Identifier (preferably a DOI or other PID; URL if no PID exists)
- Version (or date of access if unidentified)
- Type (bracketed description, e.g., [Computer software], for styles like APA)

If a describing article exists, cite it as an additional reference — not instead of the software itself.

**Preprint references:** Mark preprints clearly, including the word "preprint" or the preprint server name, with the preprint DOI. If a preprint has since been formally published, cite the formal publication instead. Preprints central to the work or covering crucial unpublished developments may still be referenced.

**Reference management software:** Many journals have templates in CSL-supporting products (e.g., Mendeley Reference Manager). Select the relevant journal template if available for automatic formatting; remove field codes before submission. If no template exists, follow the format given in this guide.

## Submitting Your Manuscript

### Submission Checklist

- One author designated as corresponding author, with full, current contact details (eligibility for a publishing agreement is based on this author's affiliation).
- All files uploaded, including artwork, videos, and supplementary materials (including tables, footnotes, and captions).
- Spelling and grammar checks carried out.
- All in-text references appear in the reference list, and vice versa.
- Permission obtained for use of any copyrighted material from other sources, including the web.
- For open access articles, all authors understand responsibility for the Article Publishing Charge (APC) if accepted (may be covered by institution or funder).

### Submit Online

The online submission system guides authors through entering manuscript details and uploading files, converting them to a single PDF for peer review. Editable files (Word, LaTeX) are required for final typesetting. All correspondence (editor's decision, revision requests) is sent by email.
